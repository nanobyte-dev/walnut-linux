1. Get our image reproducible via build script. Script should be based on a makefile and cached so it is fast. Swichover to these images. Any fixes from now on are done on the reproducible image, not on the live image.

2. GPU driver and game support. Gold standard is stable 60fps.
**Strong candidates** (GL 1.x/2.1, light, with a big-endian history from PowerPC Macs):
- **OpenArena / ioquake3**: the Quake III engine, which ran on G4/G5 Macs. A classic GPU test, and it should hit 60 fps at 1080p. RAM use is roughly 100–150 MB, which is tight but plausible.
- **QuakeSpasm** (Quake 1): GL 1.x, tiny footprint, PPC-friendly.
- **Yamagi Quake II**, with its `gl1` renderer: light and portable.
- **Extreme Tux Racer**: GL 1.x and simple, a nice controller game.
- **Neverball / Neverputt**: light, uses a gamepad well, packaged in Debian.
- **Armagetron Advanced**: very light GL, good for long-running stability tests.
- **Sauerbraten (Cube 2)**: a modest engine with a built-in map editor; a step up in GPU load.

**Stretch goals** (may work, but RAM or CPU will hurt):
- **SuperTuxKart**, in its legacy GL 2.1 renderer mode. The assets are big, so memory will be the problem.
- **Luanti (Minetest)**: has a GL 2.1 driver, but the Lua and mesh work is CPU-heavy on the PPE, and it's a heavier ClassiCube.
- **PrBoom+**, with its GL renderer: easy. It's a light test of the 2D/texture paths more than real 3D.

**Probably out of reach:**
- GL 3.x+ only: GZDoom, Warzone 2100, 0 A.D. (also far too heavy)
